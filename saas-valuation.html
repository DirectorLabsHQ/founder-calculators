How This SaaS Valuation Calculator Works

Private SaaS companies are usually valued as a multiple of ARR (annual recurring revenue). This calculator picks a starting multiple from your growth rate, adjusts it for net revenue retention, and shows a range of 25% below to 25% above the result, because real multiples vary widely deal to deal.

Formula: Enterprise value = ARR × base multiple (by growth) × NRR adjustment

Worked Example

A SaaS company has $1,000,000 ARR, grows 80% year over year, and has 115% NRR.

  1. 80% growth falls in the 50–99% tier, so the base multiple is 6×.
  2. 115% NRR earns a +10% adjustment, so 6 × 1.10 = 6.6×.
  3. Midpoint value: $1,000,000 × 6.6 = $6.6M.
  4. Range (±25%): about $5.0M to $8.3M.

What Moves a SaaS Multiple

  • Growth rate: faster, durable growth earns a higher multiple. See what changes from $50k to $250k MRR.
  • Net revenue retention: customers who stay and expand are the strongest quality signal. Compare yours with churn benchmarks by stage.
  • Efficiency: a good Rule of 40 score and a healthy CAC payback period support a premium.
  • Scale and risk: larger ARR, low customer concentration and clean financials all reduce a buyer's risk.

SaaS Valuation FAQ

What is a good ARR multiple for a SaaS company?

For private SaaS in 2026, roughly 3–7× ARR is typical, with a median around 4–5×. Top performers with high growth, strong retention and good efficiency have been reported at 7–9×.

Is this valuation or enterprise value?

It estimates enterprise value, which is the value of the business before debt and cash. Equity value to shareholders is enterprise value minus debt plus cash.

Why is my estimate lower than what I read online?

Many older articles quote 2021 multiples of 10× or more. Multiples have compressed since then, and public-company multiples are usually higher than what small private companies receive.

Does this work for early-stage startups with little revenue?

Not well. Below about $1M ARR, valuations depend more on team, market and traction than on a revenue multiple. Try the Berkus Method calculator for pre-revenue companies.
